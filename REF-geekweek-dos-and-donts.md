# Geek Week — The Do's and Don'ts

How testing works, what carries you through the week, and what trips people
up. Read it before Monday. Reread it whenever the week gets loud.

## How testing works

- **15 tasks, in order.** Task 1 before Task 2, no skipping ahead.
- **The whiteboard is the queue.** Ready to test? Write your name and task
  number on the sign-up board. When your turn comes, you test.
- **Testing happens on the big screen**, in front of the cycle. Everyone
  learns from every attempt — including yours.
- **Every attempt is logged** — name, time, result, attempt count. Your
  effort is visible all week, and that's a good thing.
- **Fail an attempt?** Take the buffer period, then sign up again at the
  end of the queue. A fail costs you a spot in line, never respect.
- **Three fails on one task?** You advance to the next task. Anything
  unfinished comes back around after Task 15.
- **Attendance during testing blocks is mandatory.** Blocks run up to
  90 minutes; the schedule lives on the cycle's Outlook calendar.

## Do

- **Sign up early and often.** The whiteboard rewards the brave — waiting
  for perfect readiness is how a day disappears.
- **Type every query by hand.** That's the rule, and it's also the training.
- **End every query with a semicolon** and keep your formatting clean —
  one clause per line reads like a pro wrote it.
- **Explain your logic out loud** — during your attempt and after. Being
  able to say *why* is graded, not just *what*.
- **Cheer every pass. Loudly.** When someone clears a task, the whole
  cycle claps. You'll want that noise when it's your turn.
- **Aim for three tasks a day.** That pace lands all 15 by Friday with
  room to breathe.
- **Use the buffer well.** Between attempts: reread your query, hit the
  resources, ask a facilitator, walk the arrows again.
- **Ask about extra testing time** if you're pushing to finish — lunch,
  dinner, all-skate, self-directed time can all become testing time when
  it works with the program team's schedule.
- **Erase your name after your attempt** — pass or fail. Leave the board
  clean for the next person.

## Don't

- **Don't copy-paste.** No pasted queries, subqueries, or stored
  procedures — ever. Hand-typed only.
- **Don't borrow a teammate's query.** Identical queries across interns
  get noticed, and they turn into a live walkthrough with different table
  names. Write your own — helping each other *understand* is encouraged;
  cloning answers is not.
- **Don't test out of order** or bank on skipping — the sequence is
  the curriculum.
- **Don't let one fail set your mood.** The buffer-and-requeue loop is
  the design, not a punishment. Everyone eats a fail this week.
- **Don't wait until Thursday.** Three a day is calm; seven on Friday
  is not.
- **Don't disappear during testing blocks.** Watching attempts is how
  half the learning happens.
- **Don't rush the semicolon-less, one-line mess.** A passing query you
  can't read or explain is only half a pass in every way that matters.

## What gets graded

| Lens | The question |
| --- | --- |
| **Accuracy** | Does it return the right results? Row counts and columns checked. |
| **Efficiency** | Right tools for the job — joins and filters, not clever complexity. |
| **Readability** | Clean, well-structured code. It reflects professionalism. |
| **Comprehension** | Can you explain your logic and approach, out loud? |

## The finish line

Completing all 15 tasks by the end of the week is **the challenge and the
expectation** of Geek Week. Finishing signals effort, engagement,
persistence, initiative, problem solving, and time management.

It is not the only thing that counts. Comprehension, participation,
adaptability, and demonstrated learning are weighed right alongside it —
a week of honest attempts, visible growth, and good questions is a strong
week. What's measured is how you climb, not just where you land.

## What's expected of you

- Participate actively in every testing opportunity
- Show consistent effort throughout the week
- Use the resources and support available to you
- Ask for help when you need it
- Make meaningful progress through the tasks
- Engage professionally with peers and staff

Geek Week is built to challenge you — and to prove to you that
accountability, persistence, and pressure are things you can handle.
