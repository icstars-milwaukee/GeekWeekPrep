10:10 · The map

A well-organized
spreadsheet system

Table
One sheet: all the customers. All the orders.
Row
One record: a single customer.
Column
One attribute: their city. Their phone.
Primary key
The ID that makes a row unique. Nobody else gets it.
Foreign key
A column that points at another table's primary key. That pointer is the relationship.
The big idea
Keep each fact once, and point. The customer's name lives in one place — 31 orders just point at it.
How Northwind connects

The map

Orders.CustomerID ──────► Customers.CustomerID ← who placed it [Order Details].OrderID ──────► Orders.OrderID ← what's in the box [Order Details].ProductID ──────► Products.ProductID ← what it cost Products.CategoryID ──────► Categories.CategoryID ← what kind of thing Products.SupplierID ──────► Suppliers.SupplierID ← who ships it to us

Read it out loud with me: one customer, many orders. One order, many line items. You don't memorize this — you learn to walk the arrows. Where would you look to find what a customer bought?

The whole language, almost

Six words do
most of the work

SELECT · FROM · WHERE
Pick the columns. Name the table. Filter the rows. This answers simple questions.
JOIN · GROUP BY · ORDER BY
Connect the tables. Summarize the piles. Sort the answer. This answers interesting ones.

Watch for all six in the next ten minutes.

I do · step 1 of 4 — my screen share, just watch. You type at 11:00

I open the door

I open SSMS → Connect → Server name: .\SQLEXPRESS → Windows Authentication. Then: New Query. I TYPE: USE Northwind; I press F5 (Execute). THE SCREEN SAYS: Commands completed successfully.

While it runs, I say: "That line points my query window at the right database — like walking into the right building before you start knocking on doors. From here on, every question I ask, Northwind answers."

I do · step 2 of 4 — select, from, where

I ask a simple question

"Which customers are in Germany?" I TYPE: SELECT CompanyName, City FROM Customers WHERE Country = 'Germany'; THE SCREEN SAYS (bottom of the grid): 11 rows

I say, reading each line: "Give me the company and the city… from the customers table… but only the German ones. Three lines, plain English with the vowels removed. 91 customers became 11 answers."

I do · step 3 of 4 — order by, and one honest mistake

I sort the answer

"What are our most expensive products?" I TYPE (and I forget the DESC on purpose): SELECT TOP 10 ProductName, UnitPrice FROM Products ORDER BY UnitPrice DESC; THE SCREEN SAYS (top row): Côte de Blaye 263.50

I say: "First time I run it without DESC, I get the ten cheapest — the data did exactly what I said, not what I meant. That's every bug you'll ever meet. I read my query again, add DESC, rerun. No panic, just reread."

I do · step 4 of 4 — join, group by

I ask a real question

"Which customers order the most?" — that needs TWO tables. I TYPE: SELECT c.CompanyName, COUNT(*) AS OrderCount FROM Orders o JOIN Customers c ON c.CustomerID = o.CustomerID GROUP BY c.CompanyName ORDER BY OrderCount DESC; THE SCREEN SAYS (top of the list): Save-a-Lot Markets 31

I say, walking the arrows: "Orders only knows the customer's ID — the JOIN walks the arrow to Customers to pick up the name. Then GROUP BY makes 830 orders into 89 piles, and ORDER BY puts the biggest pile on top. One plain-English question, one answer. That is the whole job."

I do · what you just watched

Two moves. That's the language.

Move 1: filter — SELECT, FROM, WHERE cut a big table down to the rows you care about. Move 2: summarize — JOIN, GROUP BY, ORDER BY connect tables and turn thousands of rows into one answer. At 11:00 you build a query yourself, with me, one line at a time.

We do · step 2 of 4 — start simple

Start with the products

YOU TYPE, then F5: SELECT ProductName FROM Products; THE SCREEN SAYS (bottom of the grid): 77 rows

Chai, Chang, Aniseed Syrup… every product Northwind sells, names only. The middle of the map — both arrows we need start here. Checkpoint: type your row count in the chat — a wall of 77s.

We do · step 3 of 4 — walk the first arrow

Join in the suppliers

Who ships each one to us? Add a JOIN — YOU TYPE, then F5: SELECT s.CompanyName, p.ProductName FROM Products p JOIN Suppliers s ON s.SupplierID = p.SupplierID; THE SCREEN SAYS (top row, and 77 rows at the bottom): Exotic Liquids Chai

Products only knows the supplier's ID — the JOIN walks the arrow to Suppliers and picks up the company name. Still 77 rows: same products, now wearing their supplier's name. Checkpoint: Exotic Liquids next to Chai. 👍 reaction.

We do · step 4 of 4 — second arrow, then sort

Join the categories, sort the catalog

Same move again, plus a sort — YOU TYPE, then F5: SELECT s.CompanyName, p.ProductName, c.CategoryName FROM Products p JOIN Suppliers s ON s.SupplierID = p.SupplierID JOIN Categories c ON c.CategoryID = p.CategoryID ORDER BY c.CategoryName; THE SCREEN SAYS (top rows, 77 rows at the bottom): Exotic Liquids Chai Beverages Exotic Liquids Chang Beverages

Checkpoint: type your top product in the chat — a wall of Chai. Three tables, two arrows, one sorted catalog — ORDER BY sorts A→Z unless you say DESC. The second JOIN was the same move as the first: you already knew how to do this.
